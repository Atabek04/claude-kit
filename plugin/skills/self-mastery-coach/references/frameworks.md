## 1. Implementation Intentions

**Author:** Peter Gollwitzer, 1999
**Core Insight:** Vague goals fail because they leave the moment of action undefined. If-Then plans pre-load the decision, so execution becomes automatic, not effortful.
**Structure:** "IF [specific situation/cue], THEN I will [specific behavior]."
**Research:** Meta-analysis of 94 studies — forming an if-then plan significantly increases goal attainment across exercise, diet, health screenings, voting, and more. Effect size: ~d = 0.65.
**Coaching Use:** Take every vague goal and convert it. "I'll meditate more" → "If I sit down for breakfast, I will meditate for 5 minutes before eating."
**Limitation:** Works best for discrete, low-complexity actions. Less effective for long chains of behavior requiring sustained motivation.

## 2. Atomic Habits — 4 Laws

**Author:** James Clear, 2018 (building on BJ Fogg, Duhigg, and habit loop research)
**Core Insight:** You don't rise to your goals, you fall to your systems. Identity shapes behavior — become the person who does the thing, don't just target the outcome.

**4 Laws:**
1. Make it obvious — environment design, habit stacking ("After [habit], I will [new habit]")
2. Make it attractive — temptation bundling (pair the thing you must do with something you want)
3. Make it easy — 2-Minute Rule: reduce any habit to under 2 minutes to start
4. Make it satisfying — immediate reward, habit tracking

**2-Minute Rule:** "Not 'run 5km.' Put on your shoes. Not 'write 500 words.' Open the document."
**Identity Shift:** "Every action is a vote for the type of person you want to become."
**Coaching Use:** When motivation fails, don't look for more motivation — look at the environment and the friction. Reduce friction first.

## 3. WOOP / Mental Contrasting

**Author:** Gabriele Oettingen (Mental Contrasting with Implementation Intentions, MCII)
**Core Insight:** Pure positive visualization ("imagine success") decreases motivation. Pure realism ("think about obstacles") stalls action. WOOP combines both.

**Steps:**
1. **Wish** — Define a meaningful, feasible goal
2. **Outcome** — Vividly imagine the best result
3. **Obstacle** — Identify the main *internal* barrier (not external — your own thoughts, emotions, habits)
4. **Plan** — If-Then rule for each obstacle

**Research:** WOOP participants were twice as physically active as controls. MCII showed 30% improvement in diet. Effect consistent across health, academic, and interpersonal goals.
**Coaching Use:** Before generating any plan for a user, run them through WOOP. The obstacle step alone is where most plans fail — users skip it entirely.

## 4. Planning Fallacy

**Author:** Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky, 1979
**Core Insight:** People consistently underestimate task duration even knowing that past similar tasks took longer. Driven by the "inside view" — imagining only the best-case scenario for the current project.

**Solutions:**
- Outside View: compare to base rate of similar projects. "How long did the last similar thing take?"
- Pre-Mortem: imagine the project has failed 6 months from now. What went wrong?
- Time Buffers: multiply estimates by 1.5–2x
- Backwards Planning: start from the deadline, work backwards to today

**Coaching Use:** When a user presents an ambitious plan, ask: "What would have to be true for this to go as planned — and when has that actually been true for you?" Use the pre-mortem to surface hidden risks.

## 5. Temporal Motivation Theory

**Author:** Piers Steel, 2007
**Formula:** Motivation = (Expectancy × Value) / (1 + Impulsiveness × Delay)
**Core Insight:** As the deadline gets further away, motivation collapses — regardless of how important the task is. The brain systematically underweights future rewards vs. immediate ones.

**Coaching Use:**
- Create intermediate checkpoints to manufacture short-term rewards
- Break long projects into micro-milestones with near-term deadlines
- Use streaks, habit tracking, or check-ins to reduce psychological delay
- "If you can't make the deadline feel close, make the reward feel immediate."

## 6. Self-Compassion

**Author:** Kristin Neff, 2003+
**Three Components:** Self-kindness (not self-judgment), Common humanity (others struggle too), Mindfulness (balanced awareness of pain)
**Core Insight:** Self-criticism after failure decreases motivation to retry. Self-compassion increases it.

**Research:** Students coached to be self-compassionate after a failed test studied more for the next one than students given self-esteem boosts or exposed to self-criticism. Effect replicated across multiple studies.
**Common Myth:** Self-compassion = low standards / complacency. Research shows the opposite — self-compassionate people are more motivated to improve and less afraid of failure.
**Coaching Use:** When a user reports a failure or shame spiral, validate before problem-solving. One sentence: "That sounds genuinely hard." Then diagnose. Never skip this step with a struggling user.

## 7. CBT ABCDE Model

**Author:** Albert Ellis (Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy)
**Structure:**
- **A** — Activating event ("I missed the gym")
- **B** — Belief about the event ("I'm a failure, I'll never be consistent")
- **C** — Consequence — emotional/behavioral ("I gave up entirely")
- **D** — Disputing the belief ("Is 'I'll never be consistent' actually true? What's the evidence?")
- **E** — Effective new belief ("Missing one day doesn't define my consistency — patterns do")

**Coaching Use:** When a user collapses into all-or-nothing thinking or catastrophizing, walk them through ABCDE. The key is D — helping them dispute the catastrophic interpretation without dismissing the difficulty.

## 8. Non-Zero Day Rule

**Origin:** Reddit r/getdisciplined — "No More Zero Days" by ryans01 (one of Reddit's most upvoted self-improvement posts)
**Core Insight:** A zero day — a day where you did nothing — is the only failure. One sentence, one pushup, one page, one minute: not zero.

**Quote:** "Didn't do anything all fucking day and it's 11:58 PM? Write one sentence. One pushup. Read one page of that chapter. One. Because one is non-zero."
**Why It Works:** It makes failure structurally impossible as long as the user is willing to engage for 60 seconds. Removes the perfectionism trap entirely.
**Companion principle:** The three-you framework — forgive Past You, give Present You one action, make Future You proud.
**Coaching Use:** This is the safety net. When every other framework collapses — return here. One non-zero action.

## 9. Ego Depletion / Decision Fatigue

**Original Author:** Roy Baumeister et al., 1998
**Status:** The glucose mechanism proposed by Baumeister failed large-scale replication (2016 multi-lab, 2141 participants: no ego depletion effect found). However, the practical experience of cognitive fatigue and decision overload remains well-documented.

**What Actually Holds:**
- Decision fatigue: after many decisions, quality degrades (judges give harsher rulings before lunch)
- Cognitive load: working memory is limited; multitasking drains it
- Motivation depletion: not willpower per se, but the motivation to exert effort fades

**Coaching Use (nuanced):** Don't tell users willpower is finite as an excuse. Instead: design choices to be low-friction. Schedule high-priority habits early. Reduce the number of daily decisions. Prepare the environment in advance.

## 10. Motivational Interviewing

**Author:** William Miller & Stephen Rollnick, 1991
**Core Insight:** Telling people to change doesn't work. Eliciting their own reasons to change does. Ambivalence is normal — exploring it is more effective than fighting it.

**Four Processes:** Engaging → Focusing → Evoking → Planning
**Key Technique:** Elicit "change talk" — have the user articulate their own desire, ability, reason, and need to change.

**Coaching Use:** Before giving any framework, ask: "What have you already tried? What part of this matters most to you?" This is not small talk — it activates intrinsic motivation and prevents resistance. Never impose a solution the user didn't come to themselves.

## 11. The Advice Paradox

**Author:** Scott Berkun, 2015 (also referenced widely in r/selfimprovement)
**Core Insight:** Advice feels helpful to receive but rarely leads to action. Consuming more productivity content creates the dopamine of progress without any actual change. The advice list grows; behavior doesn't.

**Coaching Use:** When a user reports consuming lots of self-improvement content without improvement, this is the diagnosis. The intervention: pick one concept, one implementation, one week. No new content until the action is done.

## 12. Collector's Fallacy

**Origin:** Zettelkasten community / PKM literature
**Core Insight:** Collecting information (notes, highlights, bookmarks) feels like learning but isn't. The brain rewards the act of saving, not the act of processing. Hoarded notes decay in value and usefulness.

**Coaching Use:** When a user describes spending more time on their note system than on actual output, diagnose Collector's Fallacy. Intervention: mandate output — one processed note before any new input. Constrain the tool.

## 13. Fight Theory — Resistance Diagnostic

**Framework:** Diagnosing the domain of resistance before prescribing a solution.

**Five Domains:**
1. **Cognitive** — Confusion about the strategy or first step ("I don't know how to start")
2. **Emotional** — Fear, shame, identity threat ("What if I fail")
3. **Behavioral** — Environmental friction, missing triggers ("The gym is too far")
4. **Motivational** — Misalignment with values ("I don't actually care about this goal")
5. **Threshold / Capacity** — Genuine depletion requiring rest, not pushing through

**Routing Logic:**
- Cognitive → clarify the first step, use IF-THEN
- Emotional → self-compassion, ABCDE, reframe
- Behavioral → environment design, habit stacking, 2-minute rule
- Motivational → MI, values clarification, identity shift
- Threshold → permission to rest, capacity audit

**Coaching Use:** This is the diagnostic backbone of Phase 1. Always identify the domain before prescribing a framework.

## 14. Accountability Structures

**Research basis:** Social pressure and commitment devices increase follow-through.
**Forms:**
- Social contracts — tell someone your plan with a specific date
- Flake fees — financial penalty for skipping (Beeminder, bet with a friend)
- Accountability partners — mutual check-ins
- Public commitment — announce goal publicly (mixed evidence — can backfire)

**Research caveat:** Public commitment sometimes substitutes for actual achievement (identity reward without behavioral change). Prefer private contracts with real consequences.
**Coaching Use:** When willpower-based approaches have repeatedly failed, accountability structures are the structural fix. "Make the habit mandatory, not optional."

## 15. Identity-Based Change

**Author:** James Clear (Atomic Habits), building on William Miller and self-concept research
**Core Insight:** Goal-based change focuses on what you want to achieve. Identity-based change focuses on who you want to become. Identity is more durable than goals because it shapes every micro-decision.

**Reframe pattern:**
- Don't: "I want to run a marathon"
- Do: "I am someone who runs"
- Every action is a vote for or against that identity

**Coaching Use:** When a user's motivation is inconsistent, check if the habit is connected to identity. "Does skipping this feel like a contradiction to who you are — or just a missed task?" If the latter: identity work first.
