# Struggle Diagnosis Reference

Use this to route from the user's described struggle to the correct domain and framework(s).

---

## Step 1 — Identify the Domain

Ask one question that separates these five domains, then match the answer to one of them:

| Domain | What It Looks Like | Example User Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive** | Unclear first step, confusion about strategy | "I don't know where to start" / "The plan feels too big" |
| **Emotional** | Fear, shame, identity threat, anxiety | "What if I fail anyway" / "I feel like a fraud" |
| **Behavioral** | Environmental friction, missing cues, competing habits | "I just forget" / "The environment makes it hard" |
| **Motivational** | Goal misalignment, values disconnect | "I don't actually care about this" / "I do it for others" |
| **Threshold / Capacity** | Genuine depletion, burnout, overwhelm | "I'm exhausted before I start" / "Everything feels endless" |

---

## Step 2 — Route to Frameworks

### Cognitive → Strategy/Execution Frameworks

Primary: **Implementation Intentions** (Gollwitzer, 1999)
Support: **2-Minute Rule** (Atomic Habits), **WOOP obstacle step**
Diagnostic question: "If you had to do just the first action right now — what would that be, exactly?"
Goal: Make the first step so specific that no decision is required.

---

### Emotional → Reframing / Self-Regulation Frameworks

Primary: **Self-Compassion** (Neff), **CBT ABCDE** (Ellis)
Support: **Non-Zero Day Rule**, **Identity-Based Change**
Diagnostic question: "When you imagine starting and failing — what's the worst-case story your brain tells you?"
Goal: Surface and dispute the catastrophic belief. Restore permission to act imperfectly.

Special case — shame spiral:
1. Validate first (one sentence only)
2. Three-You reframe: forgive Past You → one action for Present You → make Future You proud
3. Then ABCDE or Non-Zero Day

---

### Behavioral → Environment/System Design Frameworks

Primary: **Atomic Habits 4 Laws** (Clear), **Habit Stacking**
Support: **Implementation Intentions** (cue design), **Accountability Structures**
Diagnostic question: "What does your environment look like when you skip this habit — where are you, what time is it, what's competing for your attention?"
Goal: Redesign the environment so the habit is the path of least resistance.

Friction audit checklist:
- [ ] Is the cue visible and obvious?
- [ ] Is the first step under 2 minutes?
- [ ] Is there a competing habit or distraction at that moment?
- [ ] Does the reward feel immediate after doing it?

---

### Motivational → Values / Identity Frameworks

Primary: **Motivational Interviewing** (Miller & Rollnick), **Identity-Based Change** (Clear)
Support: **WOOP wish step**, **Temporal Motivation Theory**
Diagnostic question: "Does this goal actually matter to *you* — or is it something you think you *should* want?"
Goal: Reconnect the habit to a value the user actually holds. If the goal is misaligned, no system will fix it — the goal needs renegotiation.

Warning: do not try to motivate someone toward a goal they don't actually want. Surface the real goal first.

---

### Threshold / Capacity → Rest and Triage Frameworks

Primary: **Burnout recognition**, **Capacity audit**
Support: **Non-Zero Day Rule** (minimum viable action), **Temporal Motivation Theory** (reduce scope)
Diagnostic question: "Is this feeling new (recent overload) or has it been building for weeks/months?"
Goal: Distinguish between needing rest (capacity problem) vs. needing structure (system problem).

Capacity audit:
- [ ] Sleep quality last 7 days?
- [ ] How many high-effort commitments are running in parallel?
- [ ] When did you last have a day with no obligations?
- [ ] Are you confusing productivity guilt with actual capacity?

If capacity is the issue: prescribe rest explicitly. "The intervention is not a new system. It is permission to stop."
